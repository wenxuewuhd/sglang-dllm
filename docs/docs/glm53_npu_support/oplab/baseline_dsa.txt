=== sweep: DSA against sequence length -- the whole point ===
  index_topk = 2048 caps the attention itself.  The indexer does not
  get a cap: it scores n/index_kpool = n/4 pools, so it is O(n).
  Nothing below changes a tensor shape; only the int32 length
  metadata the captured graph reads changes, which is how the served
  graph handles varying lengths too.

  least squares over n >= 4096 (attention saturated), 4 points:
    DSA family = 5.640 + 8.217e-07 x n   ms/step
  Two mechanisms, and the sweep separates them:
    * SparseFlashAttention grows only until it hits index_topk=2048,
      then is flat forever.  That is the cap working.
    * LightningIndexer is the O(n) term: it scores n/4 pools and
      nothing caps that.
  For scale, the multi-card TP8 line fitted the WHOLE decode step at
  27.3 + 5.4e-6 x n ms/token over 32k..1M.  This slope is the TP1 DSA
  family only -- the two are not the same quantity and the ratio
  between them is not evidence of anything on its own.
